A continuously updated probabilistic model of the individual IVF cycle — from ovarian stimulation through blastocyst formation and embryo transfer — that evolves with each new clinical observation and scores its own confidence.

Every IVF cycle is a sequence of biological events, each introducing its own uncertainty. The IVF Digital Twin does not give a single number — it gives a complete picture of realistic possibilities, updated in real time as the cycle unfolds.

The clinical problem

When counselling an IVF patient, the question is rarely just “what is the success rate?” It is: how variable is this prognosis? Could this patient end up with no transferable embryos, or is that risk genuinely small? How does this cycle compare to others we have seen? And as each day of culture brings new information, does the prognosis actually change?

Existing tools offer a single static estimate. They produce one number that does not update as embryological data accumulate, cannot reflect your laboratory’s own performance level, and have no way to express whether a “two blastocyst” prediction is reliable or highly uncertain. Two patients can share the same expected outcome but differ fundamentally in how predictable that outcome is — and current calculators cannot capture this distinction.

The IVF Digital Twin was built for exactly this purpose. It models each cycle as it unfolds, maintaining a full range of possible outcomes that narrows with each new observation. It integrates your laboratory’s own quality composite into every prediction. And in version 7.0, it scores its own confidence — telling the clinician not only what it predicts, but how much to trust that prediction for this particular patient.

How it works

The system analyses each cycle through seven independent layers, then formally combines their conclusions into a single probability estimate. No single method dominates — each contributes a different perspective, and the final answer reflects how well they agree. When the methods converge, confidence is high. When they diverge, that divergence is itself clinically informative.

Prediction tier
1
Biological cascade simulation
5,000 versions of this cycle
The system runs 5,000 simulated versions of the cycle, each following a slightly different probabilistic path through retrieval, fertilisation, blastocyst development, and implantation. The result is a full distribution of possible outcomes — not a single number but a realistic range, including the probability of poor response and empty cycles.
2
Ensemble pregnancy scoring
Two validated models, combined
Two clinically validated pregnancy prediction models are combined: one based on patient characteristics, one on laboratory performance. Together they produce a per-transfer probability, a cumulative probability across viable embryos, and an overall cycle probability.
3
Neural network with real-time updating
Pattern recognition, anchored to your clinic
A neural network ensemble recognises complex patterns between patient parameters and outcomes. Its estimate is anchored to your clinic’s own historical data via Bayesian updating, and re-conditions itself at each new stage — oocyte count, fertilisation result, blastocyst formation — as data arrive.
4
Patient phenotype classification
Where this patient fits in the population
The patient is matched to one of three clinically defined IVF response phenotypes — Poor, Standard, or High responder — based on eighteen clinical and laboratory parameters. This positions the current patient within a reference cohort and provides immediate context for the predicted outcome range.
Verification tier
5
Independent generative verification
A second, independent opinion on embryology
A generative AI model (trained on 15,193 cycles) independently reconstructs the expected embryological outcome distribution using a completely different method, without using the simulation’s biological rate assumptions. When this independent model and the simulation agree, convergence provides additional confidence. When they diverge, the system flags this and uses the degree of divergence to adjust the weight placed on the simulation.
6
Patient-similarity reasoning
Real historical patients, not statistical abstractions
A graph-based model draws on 16,000 real clinical IVF protocols to find the patients whose profiles most closely resemble the current patient. It identifies the ten most similar historical cases with their documented outcomes — a direct, intuitive answer to the question every clinician implicitly asks: “I have seen patients like this. What happened to them?”
Synthesis tier
7
Bayesian evidence fusion
One final answer, with a confidence score
All six preceding analyses are formally combined into a single posterior probability, with each source weighted by its calibration quality and the current level of agreement between models. The simulation provides a mechanistic starting point; the AI models provide empirical evidence. The final output includes a probability, a 95% uncertainty range, and a 0–100 reliability score.
Prediction confidence

Version 7.0 introduces a formal answer to the question every clinician implicitly asks: how much should I trust this prediction for this specific patient? The system scores its own reliability on a 0–100 scale and separately flags when a patient lies outside the population on which it was trained — distinguishing, for example, a patient who is clinically unusual but has entirely typical embryological results from one where it is the laboratory picture that is atypical. When methods agree, confidence is high; when they diverge, the score falls and that divergence is itself reported as clinically meaningful information.

What the clinician receives
A range, not a number
Full outcome distributions for oocyte yield, blastocyst count, and pregnancy likelihood. The system shows not just the most likely result but the complete realistic range — including the probability of both best- and worst-case outcomes for this specific patient and laboratory.
A prediction that evolves
Every new embryological result triggers a full update. The prediction after retrieval differs from the prediction after fertilisation, which differs again after blastocyst culture. Uncertainty narrows progressively, and the reliability score updates with each new observation.
Real patients, not abstractions
The system identifies ten real historical patients whose clinical profiles most closely resemble the current patient, with their documented outcomes. A concrete answer to the question: “Among patients like this one, what happened?”
Guaranteed uncertainty intervals
Blastocyst count prediction intervals carry a mathematical guarantee: 90% of real outcomes fall within the stated range, without any assumption about the underlying biology. This interval can be communicated to patients with a defined level of statistical confidence.
Embryo banking planning
For patients considering embryo accumulation, the system estimates how many stimulation cycles are likely needed to reach a banking target with 80% or 90% confidence, accounting for the individual response profile and the laboratory’s own performance level.
Structured clinical report
An exportable PDF report suitable for counselling and documentation, compiling all layer outputs, the confidence score, risk flags, phenotype classification, and the similar-patient display into a single readable summary.
Laboratory quality is built in to every prediction
Every prediction integrates a laboratory performance composite derived from oocyte yield efficiency, fertilisation rate, and blastocyst conversion relative to expected values. Two patients with identical clinical profiles treated in laboratories of different performance levels will receive different prognoses — because the laboratory is part of the outcome.

Designed for clinical use

The IVF Digital Twin requires only the data already collected as part of routine IVF care. No additional investigations, specialised imaging, or genetic testing are needed. The platform works in any IVF setting that maintains standard embryological documentation, including settings without time-lapse incubation or preimplantation genetic testing.

Every prediction can be traced to a specific stage of the embryological cascade. A physician can explain to a patient why the predicted blastocyst range is wide, why the confidence score is moderate, or why the prognosis shifted after a strong fertilisation result — because the system makes these relationships visible and quantified. This distinguishes the IVF Digital Twin from approaches that produce accurate outputs but offer no insight into the reasoning behind them.

The system is designed to support clinical judgement, not replace it. It does not make treatment decisions, prescribe protocols, or interpret imaging. Its role is to give physicians and embryologists a structured, continuously updated, and confidence-scored quantification of the outcome probabilities already implicit in the clinical data they collect — in a form suitable for both clinical decision-making and meaningful patient communication.
